The Net draws the Internet's public hubs and the real branches between them, from the sources below. The hubs and branches, as drawn here, are offered under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported licence, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/ (the parts from the Internet Topology Zoo remain under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

Submarine cables and their landing points: TeleGeography, Submarine Cable Map data as of 2021-06-29 (repository commit 85198ba3), licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/. The 2021 data is read from a fork that preserves TeleGeography's repository, https://github.com/stealthscooty/www.submarinecablemap.com. TeleGeography's live map is at https://www.submarinecablemap.com.

Backbone networks: Internet Topology Zoo (Data Set), by Matthew Roughan, Hung Nguyen and Nickolas Falkner, Adelaide University, 2026, https://doi.org/10.25909/30153949.v1, licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. The Zoo is described in S. Knight, H. X. Nguyen, N. Falkner, R. Bowden and M. Roughan, "The Internet Topology Zoo", IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications 29(9), 2011, https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2011.111002. Thirty of its backbone networks are drawn, as they were mapped up to 2012.

The nineteen places that answer this site: Vercel's regions, https://vercel.com/docs/regions.

Changes: landing points and backbone points of presence merged into hubs within 100 km; each cable drawn as a chain of stops in route order, each landing point or branching junction linked to the next (junctions within 25 km of each other on a cable merged, junctions within 100 km of a hub joined to it, dead ends and junctions where nothing branches removed); backbone adjacencies longer than 3,000 km left out as overlays on submarine routes; links deduplicated and drawn as great-circle arcs; planned cables removed; coordinates rounded to 0.1°; dotted links to Vercel's regions are schematic, not real routes. No endorsement by TeleGeography, Adelaide University or Vercel is implied; the data is provided as is.
